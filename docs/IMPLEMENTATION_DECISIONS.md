# Prospective implementation decisions

These choices were made before acquiring or fitting scientific outcomes. They are operational and methodological refinements of the supplied proposal, not claims that the project has been registered.

- Narval replaces the reference H100 with A100 40 GB hardware. Initial concurrency is 64 CPU cores and one GPU. Aggregate ceilings remain 20,000 CPU core-hours and 400 GPU-hours. Actual pilot throughput and native accounting determine admission of full phases.
- The ant primary cohort is the source paper's 2021 set of 60 brains. The 24 earlier-year ant records and 12 parasite records remain documented exclusions from that primary analysis. Twelve refusal brains stay outside molecular score discovery.
- The public GenBank GCA assembly currently lacks distributed gene annotations. Alignment therefore uses its matching RefSeq GCF genome and annotation as a pair, retaining assembly provenance and documenting departure from the original paper's gene models. Original preprocessing uses CROP97 then HEADCROP13; the available STAR and featureCounts versions are recorded.
- All three recurrent agent architectures receive twenty final seeds. A matched feedforward comparator adds sixty final policies. Development uses five seeds per architecture-task and all three common learning rates within each seed, giving 180 development fits including that comparator.
- The feedforward observation window is 32 steps, covering the largest declared held-out memory delay of 20 steps. Its parameter budget is matched to the recurrent architectures. A shorter window would confound architectural capability with unavailable cue history.
- The common agent curriculum allocates one quarter of vector environments to independently cued competence probes, with identical allocation across architectures and tasks. Held-out probe episodes remain separate. This makes the competence tests interpretable without accepting simple action emission as competence.
- Perturbation strength uses separately reported norm-matched and policy-divergence-matched arms. Achieved matching values and failures are retained. Final testing never chooses the most favorable held-out seed or strength.
- Policy training uses GPU jobs. Final frozen-policy evaluation can use CPU allocations, with backend/version provenance and small CPU/CUDA probability comparisons. This reserves GPU-hours for learning while preserving the fixed evaluation design.
- Public-source transfer is finite file management through Narval's documented transfer endpoint. Source verification, extraction, reference indexing, alignment, modelling and scheduled controllers run in compute allocations. The measured compute proxy does not reach every source repository.
- The computational H4 test and biological transfer have separate evidence requirements. Behavioural transition, gain and drift estimates do not identify signs in neural weight subspaces. H4 can test a frozen computational operating region with synthetic interventions; a biological signature or class-prior mapping remains a conditional, separately frozen bridge. No four neural signs are invented from insect movement fits, and synthetic results never establish an insect mechanism.
- Ant–fly molecular transfer uses matching FlyBase FB2026_03 protein, gene and GO files and actual two-species orthology. Its ant score and nuisance descriptors freeze before screen gene outcomes are exported. Mantis ORFs and three-species corroboration use a separate namespace and cannot change that primary freeze.
- Native DESeq2 1.46.0 rejects normal-prior shrinkage on a no-intercept design. Primary Wald contrasts retain the original no-intercept MLE model. Secondary shrunken effects use an equivalent intercept-coded mean model with DESeq2's expanded categorical matrix and empirical normal prior; their reported uncertainty is posterior SD. Both ant and mantis production branches passed native synthetic qualification before biological fitting.
